EVERGARDEN - MAGNIFYING GLASS INFO TEXT AND SOURCES
====================================================

Every piece of real-world information shown in the Magnifying Glass info window, each followed by the source link it comes from.
Sources accessed on 4 October 2026.

Two sources are used:
  1. Encyclopaedia Britannica: every fact. Crops, eggs and chickens use the main encyclopedia; goat, horse and sheep use Britannica for Students.
  2. University of Minnesota Extension, "Crop and field planning tools for vegetable farmers": only the days-to-harvest numbers on each crop's Farm tab.

The info window's "Growth" and "Game" tabs aren't listed. They're generated from the game's own settings and make no real-world claims.

CROPS
====================================================

----------------------------------------------------
TOMATO
----------------------------------------------------
[About]
Surprise! A tomato is really a fruit, a juicy berry full of tiny seeds.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/tomato

[Farm]
On a real farm: ready in about 65 to 85 days after planting outside (50 to 70 for cherry tomatoes). Farmers stake or cage the plants to keep the fruit off the ground!
  Source (days): https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/specialty-crops/vegetable-farming/crop-and-field-planning-tools-for-vegetable-farmers
  Source (staking): https://www.britannica.com/plant/tomato

[Fact 1]
Wild tomatoes first grew in the Andes Mountains of South America!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/tomato

[Fact 2]
The word tomato comes from tomatl, a word in the Aztec language.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/tomato

[Fact 3]
Tomatoes aren't only red. They can be yellow, green or even purple!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/tomato

[Fact 4]
Tomatoes are cousins of eggplants! Their leaves are poisonous, so only eat the fruit.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/tomato

CORN
----------------------------------------------------
[About]
Corn is actually a giant grass! Each ear is wrapped up in leafy husks.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/corn-plant

[Farm]
On a real farm: ready in about 70 to 85 days after planting the seeds.
  Source: https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/specialty-crops/vegetable-farming/crop-and-field-planning-tools-for-vegetable-farmers

[Fact 1]
People in southern Mexico first grew corn about 10,000 years ago!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/corn-plant

[Fact 2]
Each strand of corn silk leads to just one kernel!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/corn-plant

[Fact 3]
Corn kernels can be red, blue, pink or even black!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/corn-plant

[Fact 4]
Popcorn pops because heat makes the water inside each kernel expand until it bursts!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/corn-plant


----------------------------------------------------
BROCCOLI
----------------------------------------------------
[About]
Broccoli is a kind of cabbage! We eat its flower buds and stalk.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/broccoli

[Farm]
On a real farm: ready in about 55 to 65 days after planting outside. If it isn't picked, its buds open into little yellow flowers!
  Source (days): https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/specialty-crops/vegetable-farming/crop-and-field-planning-tools-for-vegetable-farmers
  Source (flowers): https://www.britannica.com/plant/broccoli

[Fact 1]
Ancient Romans grew broccoli in Italy!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/broccoli

[Fact 2]
Broccoli plants grow about 2 to 3 feet tall.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/broccoli

[Fact 3]
Broccoli is packed with vitamins A, C and K!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/broccoli


----------------------------------------------------
CAULIFLOWER
----------------------------------------------------
[About]
Cauliflower is a kind of cabbage! Its white head, called the curd, is made of flowers that haven't opened yet.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/cauliflower

[Farm]
On a real farm: ready in about 75 to 80 days after planting outside. Before harvest, farmers tie the big leaves over the head to shade it!
  Source (days): https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/specialty-crops/vegetable-farming/crop-and-field-planning-tools-for-vegetable-farmers
  Source (leaves): https://www.britannica.com/plant/cauliflower

[Fact 1]
Cauliflower isn't only white. It can be orange, purple, green or brown too!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/cauliflower

[Fact 2]
Cauliflower likes cool weather, around 60°F, to grow its head.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/cauliflower

[Fact 3]
If cauliflower gets too dry, it only grows small heads.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/cauliflower


----------------------------------------------------
CARROT
----------------------------------------------------
[About]
Carrots are roots that grow underground! The part we eat is called a taproot.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/carrot

[Farm]
On a real farm: ready in about 55 to 75 days after planting the seeds.
  Source: https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/specialty-crops/vegetable-farming/crop-and-field-planning-tools-for-vegetable-farmers

[Fact 1]
Carrots aren't only orange. They can be white, yellow or purple too!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/carrot

[Fact 2]
People probably started farming carrots in Central Asia about 1,000 years ago!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/carrot

[Fact 3]
Carrots are full of carotene, which your body turns into vitamin A!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/carrot

[Fact 4]
Wild carrots have a fancy name: Queen Anne's lace!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/plant/carrot


----------------------------------------------------
EGG
----------------------------------------------------
[About]
Fresh eggs from your hens! A hen makes one egg about every 23 to 26 hours.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/animal/chicken

[Fact 1]
Some hens can lay as many as 300 eggs a year!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/animal/chicken

[Fact 2]
Long, sunny days help hens lay more eggs.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/animal/chicken

[Fact 3]
Hens start laying eggs when they're about six months old.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/animal/chicken

[Fact 4]
If an egg is fertilized, a fluffy chick hatches about 21 days later!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/animal/chicken

ANIMALS

----------------------------------------------------
CHICKEN
----------------------------------------------------
[About]
Chickens are farm birds raised for eggs and meat. They come from the wild red jungle fowl of India!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/animal/chicken

[Fact 1]
There are more than 60 breeds of chickens!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/animal/chicken

[Fact 2]
Chickens have a pecking order, a ranking that decides who gets to eat first!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/animal/chicken

[Fact 3]
Fluffy chicks grow all their feathers in just 4 to 5 weeks!
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/animal/chicken

[Fact 4]
Free-range chickens can live 6 to 8 years.
  Source: https://www.britannica.com/animal/chicken


----------------------------------------------------
GOAT
----------------------------------------------------
[About]
Goats are amazing climbers with hollow horns! They first came from the mountains of Europe, Asia and northern Africa.
  Source: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/goat/274580

[Fact 1]
Goats are smart and social, and they like to live together in herds.
  Source: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/goat/274580

[Fact 2]
Do goats eat tin cans? Nope! That's just a myth.
  Source: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/goat/274580

[Fact 3]
Baby goats are called kids! A mom usually has one to three, and they can follow her just a few hours after they're born.
  Source: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/goat/274580

[Fact 4]
Goats can live to be about 15 years old.
  Source: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/goat/274580


----------------------------------------------------
HORSE
----------------------------------------------------
[About]
Horses are strong, plant-eating animals with hooves. For hundreds of years they've helped people plow fields, carry things and travel!
  Source: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/horse/274947

[Fact 1]
Horses only sleep about 2 to 3 hours a day, and they often nap standing up!
  Source: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/horse/274947

[Fact 2]
Horses have the biggest eyes of any animal that lives on land!
  Source: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/horse/274947

[Fact 3]
A horse's height is measured in "hands." One hand equals 4 inches!
  Source: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/horse/274947


----------------------------------------------------
SHEEP
----------------------------------------------------
[About]
Sheep give us meat, milk and wool! They stick together in flocks and usually follow a leader.
  Source: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/sheep/277023

[Fact 1]
Every spring, wool sheep get a haircut! Farmers shear off their fleece to make wool.
  Source: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/sheep/277023

[Fact 2]
Baby sheep are called lambs. They can follow their moms just a day or two after they're born!
  Source: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/sheep/277023

[Fact 3]
How do you tell sheep and goats apart? Sheep never have beards, but goats often do!
  Source: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/sheep/277023
